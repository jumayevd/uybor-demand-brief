Demand & Attention Brief · uybor.uz

Tashkent housing demand, measured in views.

A demand read on 3,531 active sale listings across greater Tashkent, tracked daily for 19 days. Demand here is attention — new views a listing earns each day — and the gap between the typical listing and the average one is where the story lives.

The mean sits 2.4× above the median because demand is extremely concentrated: the top 10% of listings capture 52.3% of all new views, while the bottom half share just 6.1%. Throughout this brief, gold marks the median (the typical home) and teal marks the mean (where the attention piles up).
01

Demand momentum over the window

Aggregate demand roughly doubled across the 19 days — from ~19k new views/day in late June to ~45k by mid-July. But the median listing’s daily gain fell toward 1 view even as the mean climbed past 18. Interest isn’t spreading; it’s funnelling onto fewer listings.

02

The shape of attention

Demand follows a power law. Most listings gather a trickle of views; a few command floods. And attention is overwhelmingly passive — of 582,979 new views, only 785 became clicks and 203 became saved favourites.

Reading it

  • Because clicks and favourites are so scarce (88.7% and 94.9% of listings get none), they are high-signal intent markers — worth weighting far more heavily than a raw view.
  • A demand index should not sum views, clicks and favourites; they measure different depths of the funnel.
03

Attention predicts who leaves the market

This is the payoff. Over the window, 37% of listings present in week one disappeared — sold, delisted, or expired. Those exiting listings were pulling ~5× the daily view velocity of the ones that stayed. Demand, read through views, is a leading indicator of a listing clearing.

Reading it

  • Median exiting listing: 12.0 views/day vs 2.3 views/day for those that stayed. The velocity gap is steep enough to use views/day as a demand-and-liquidity proxy where transaction data is missing.
  • Caveat: exit conflates sold with delisted/expired. To sharpen it, test whether exiting listings cut price in their final snapshots (weak demand) versus disappearing cleanly (likely sold).
04

Where demand concentrates

Attention and price pull apart. The pricey central districts — Mirabad, Yakkasaray — carry the highest $/m² but only middling view velocity, while Shaykhantakhur, Yangihayot and Yunusabad lead on demand per listing. High price does not mean high demand.

05

Demand across the apartment stock

Bigger homes draw more total eyeballs — 6+ room homes average ~3,100 lifetime views — yet the typical listing’s daily demand is remarkably flat across room counts. Secondary stock outdraws new builds on median velocity despite being cheaper.

06

Price versus demand

Across apartment price quintiles, view velocity is U-shaped — cheap and expensive both draw eyes, the mid-market ($107–157k) is the dead zone. But intent is cleaner: click and favourite rates fall monotonically with price. The top tier’s views are largely aspirational, not buyers.

Bottom line

  • Raw views overstate demand at the top: the priciest quintile draws 5.5 views/day but converts only 3.1% to a click vs 19.4% at the bottom.
  • The mid-market trough is real on both measures — fewest views and low intent. If you model liquidity, give it its own indicator.
  • Listed attributes explain under 5% of view variance (from a hedonic regression), which is why attention carries information beyond price, size and location — and why it predicts exit.
Methodology

Source: 47,801 daily listing snapshots scraped from uybor.uz over 24 Jun – 13 Jul 2026 (19 snapshot dates; 5 Jul missing), covering 3,531 unique for-sale listings. Demand is proxied by views and, primarily, by demand velocity — new views gained between snapshots, normalised to a per-day rate; committed demand by clicks and favourites. Velocity correlates just −0.05 with listing age, so it isolates current demand from tenure. “Exit” = a listing present in week one that is absent at the final snapshot (sold, delisted, or expired), computed only for listings observed early enough to have the chance. Prices are advertised list prices in USD, not transaction prices; price and $/m² statistics exclude entries outside \\$3k–\\$5M and areas outside 10–2,000 m². Both mean and median are reported throughout: the median describes the typical listing, the mean exposes the long attention tail. District cuts are Tashkent city with ≥25 listings. Figures are listing-side demand signals and should not be read as closed-sale statistics.
